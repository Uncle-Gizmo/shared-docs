# Retatrutide and knee osteoarthritis pain: a briefing note

**Date:** 21 November 2026  
**Subject:** The experimental weight-loss medicine retatrutide, following a ScienceDaily report that it helped patients cut knee pain in half.

This note is for general information only. It is **not medical advice** and is not a recommendation to start, stop or change any treatment. Anyone considering a weight-loss or pain medicine should talk to their GP or another qualified clinician. Figures below are taken from named sources; nothing here has been invented.

---

## 1. Which drug is it, and how does it work?

The medicine is **retatrutide** (research code **LY3437943**). It does **not** yet have a brand name, because it is still experimental and has not been licensed anywhere in the world.

It is made by Eli Lilly and is given as a **once-weekly injection under the skin**. In the trials, people started on 2 mg weekly and the dose was stepped up every four weeks to a target of 4 mg, 9 mg or 12 mg.

Retatrutide is a **triple hormone receptor agonist**. A single molecule switches on three of the body's own hormone receptors at once:

- **GLP-1** (glucagon-like peptide-1)
- **GIP** (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide)
- **glucagon**

Existing weekly injections such as semaglutide (Wegovy, Ozempic) act on GLP-1 only. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, and Zepbound in the United States) acts on GIP and GLP-1. Retatrutide adds the glucagon receptor as well. In practice this is meant to reduce appetite, slow stomach emptying and increase energy use, which is why the weight loss in trials has been larger than with the one- and two-receptor medicines.

Lilly and others sometimes see it nicknamed “GLP-3”. Lilly says that label is scientifically inaccurate. The accurate description is a triple agonist, sometimes called a “triple G” medicine.

**Main sources:** [Lilly, “What to know about retatrutide” (updated October 2026)](https://www.lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide); [Schnitzer et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 8 November 2026](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2610676); [Drugs.com retatrutide monograph (updated 30 October 2026)](https://www.drugs.com/retatrutide.html).

## 2. The trials: who took part, the dose, how long they ran, and the results

The ScienceDaily article of 19 November 2026 is based on a [Northwestern University news release of 8 November 2026](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2026/11/experimental-weight-loss-drug-eases-painful-knee-osteoarthritis-in-two-clinical-trials). The underlying research is a single *New England Journal of Medicine* paper covering **two phase 3 trials**, both funded by Lilly:

| Trial | ClinicalTrials.gov | Length | Doses tested | People randomised |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TRIUMPH-4 | [NCT05931367](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05931367) | 68 weeks | 9 mg or 12 mg weekly, or placebo | 445 |
| TRIUMPH-1-OA | [NCT05929066](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05929066) | 80 weeks | 4 mg, 9 mg or 12 mg weekly, or placebo | 574 |

Together that is just over 1,000 people. Both trials enrolled **adults without diabetes** who had **painful knee osteoarthritis** and a **body-mass index (BMI) of at least 27**. In TRIUMPH-4, people also needed a baseline WOMAC pain score between 4 and 9 (the scale runs from 0 to 10, with higher scores meaning worse pain). Lilly reported that the average person in TRIUMPH-4 started at 112.7 kg (BMI 40.4) with a WOMAC pain score of 6.0, and that 84.0% had a BMI of 35 or more. A later summary of both published trials put the share with a BMI above 35 at about 80%. People who already needed a knee replacement were excluded.

The main pain measure was the **WOMAC pain score** (Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Arthritis Index). Function was measured on the WOMAC physical function score.

Two ways of analysing the same data appear in the sources, and they should not be mixed up:

- The **treatment-regimen / intention-to-treat style figures** include people who stopped the injections. These are the headline numbers in the *NEJM* paper and are the more conservative set.
- The **efficacy estimand** (used in Lilly’s earlier 11 December 2025 TRIUMPH-4 announcement) asks what would have happened if everyone had stayed on the planned treatment. Those numbers are larger.

ScienceDaily’s phrase “about 25% of their body weight at the highest dose” is a rounded figure. The published *NEJM* numbers are a little lower or higher depending on the trial and the analysis, as set out below.

### Knee pain

In **TRIUMPH-4 at 68 weeks**, using the paper’s hybrid-treatment analysis, mean WOMAC pain scores fell by:

- **9 mg:** −3.96 points
- **12 mg:** −3.74 points
- **Placebo:** −2.12 points

The differences versus placebo were −1.83 and −1.61 points respectively (both statistically significant). The intention-to-treat differences were smaller but still significant: −1.70 (9 mg) and −1.38 (12 mg).

In **TRIUMPH-1-OA at 80 weeks**, the hybrid-treatment falls were −3.22 (4 mg), −3.48 (9 mg) and −3.64 (12 mg), versus −1.87 with placebo. All comparisons with placebo were statistically significant.

When the two trials were pooled, **about two-thirds of people on retatrutide had their knee pain cut by at least half**. The published pooled figures at week 68 were:

- **9 mg:** 69.6% (189 of 248)
- **12 mg:** 63.2% (167 of 236)
- **Placebo:** 37.8% (86 of 185)

The 9 mg dose was at least as good as 12 mg on several pain measures. Lilly’s earlier efficacy analysis of TRIUMPH-4 also reported that 14.1% (9 mg) and 12.0% (12 mg) were completely free of knee pain at 68 weeks, compared with 4.2% on placebo; that was a post-hoc finding and was not a pre-specified primary end point.

Physical function also improved more than with placebo in both trials.

### Weight

**TRIUMPH-4 at 68 weeks** (treatment-regimen figures, matching the *NEJM* paper):

- **9 mg:** −20.0% (−22.9 kg)
- **12 mg:** −23.7% (−27.2 kg)
- **Placebo:** −4.6% (−5.3 kg)

Lilly’s efficacy estimand for the same trial (people who stayed on treatment) was higher: **−26.4% at 9 mg and −28.7% (−32.3 kg, or 71.2 lb) at 12 mg**, versus −2.1% with placebo. On that analysis, 58.6% of people on 12 mg lost at least 25% of their body weight, and 39.4% lost at least 30%.

**TRIUMPH-1-OA at 80 weeks:** mean weight change ranged from **−17.5% at 4 mg to −24.8% at 12 mg**, versus **−4.3% with placebo**. The differences versus placebo were −13.2, −18.6 and −20.6 percentage points at 4 mg, 9 mg and 12 mg.

Blood pressure, blood fats and a marker of inflammation (hsCRP) also improved. In TRIUMPH-4, Lilly reported a 14.0 mmHg fall in systolic blood pressure at the highest dose on the efficacy analysis.

The investigators think most of the pain relief comes from taking load off the knee as weight falls. They also suspect an anti-inflammatory effect, but they say the mechanism is not fully known and needs more research.

**Main sources:** [Schnitzer et al., *NEJM*, 8 November 2026](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2610676); [ScienceDaily, 19 November 2026](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261118065534.htm); [Northwestern Now, 8 November 2026](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2026/11/experimental-weight-loss-drug-eases-painful-knee-osteoarthritis-in-two-clinical-trials); [Lilly TRIUMPH-4 press release, 11 December 2025](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-weight-loss-average); [Rheumatology Live summary of the *NEJM* paper](https://www.rheum-live.com/view/retatrutide-reduces-knee-osteoarthritis-pain-in-triumph-4-and-triumph-1-oa).

## 3. Side effects

There is no licensed UK or US product information yet, so the figures below come from the trials, not from an official patient leaflet.

The most common side effects were **digestive**, usually mild to moderate, and mostly appeared while the dose was being stepped up:

In **TRIUMPH-4**, Lilly reported these rates for 9 mg and 12 mg versus placebo:

| Side effect | 9 mg | 12 mg | Placebo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nausea | 38.1% | 43.2% | 10.7% |
| Diarrhoea | 34.7% | 33.1% | 13.4% |
| Constipation | 21.8% | 25.0% | 8.7% |
| Vomiting | 20.4% | 20.9% | 0.0% |
| Decreased appetite | 19.0% | 18.2% | 9.4% |

**Dysesthesia** — an unusual or unpleasant skin sensation such as tingling, burning or sensitivity to touch — was clearly more common with retatrutide than is typical for similar licensed medicines. In TRIUMPH-4 it occurred in **8.8% on 9 mg and 20.9% on 12 mg**, versus **0.7% on placebo**. Across both knee-pain trials the published range was **6.3% to 20.9%** versus 0.7% with placebo. Lilly said these events were generally mild and rarely led to people stopping the drug.

People who stopped because of side effects:

- **TRIUMPH-4:** 12.8% (9 mg) and 18.2% (12 mg), versus 4.0% on placebo. (Lilly’s earlier topline figure for 9 mg was 12.2%; the published paper figure is 12.8%.) Lilly noted that some of these stops were for **perceived excessive weight loss**, and that rates were lower in people who started with a BMI of 35 or more (8.8% and 12.1% versus 4.8% on placebo).
- **TRIUMPH-1-OA:** 2.8% to 15.3% on retatrutide, versus 6.2% on placebo.

Stops specifically because of digestive side effects in TRIUMPH-4 were 6.1% to 8.1% on retatrutide versus 1.3% on placebo; in TRIUMPH-1-OA they were 1.4% to 5.6% versus none on placebo.

The long-term safety of retatrutide has not been established. It has not yet been used as a licensed medicine in routine care.

**Main sources:** [Lilly TRIUMPH-4 press release, 11 December 2025](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-weight-loss-average); [Schnitzer et al., *NEJM*, 8 November 2026](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2610676); [Rheumatology Live](https://www.rheum-live.com/view/retatrutide-reduces-knee-osteoarthritis-pain-in-triumph-4-and-triumph-1-oa); [Drugs.com, 30 October 2026](https://www.drugs.com/retatrutide.html).

## 4. Dangers, serious risks, and who should not take it

**Serious adverse events were more common with retatrutide than with placebo.** In TRIUMPH-4 they occurred in **9.5% and 10.8%** of the retatrutide groups versus **2.0%** on placebo. Two deaths occurred in TRIUMPH-4, one in each retatrutide group; the investigators judged both **unrelated to treatment**. There were no deaths in TRIUMPH-1-OA.

Important unanswered safety questions include:

- **Muscle loss.** The Northwestern investigators stressed that medicines in this family can cause loss of muscle as well as fat. Muscle around the knee helps to stabilise the joint, so losing too much muscle could be unhelpful for people with osteoarthritis. Diet, exercise and maintaining muscle remain important.
- **Gallbladder problems, pancreatitis, severe gut slowing, dehydration and (rarely) aspiration under anaesthetic** are recognised risks of the wider GLP-1 family. Specific rates for those events in these two knee-pain trials were not clearly itemised in the public summaries reviewed for this note, so they are not quoted here.
- **Heart events.** A later obesity trial in people with existing heart disease (TRIUMPH-3) did not prove that retatrutide reduces heart attacks or strokes. A dedicated heart-outcomes trial is still running.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding.** Retatrutide has not been shown to be safe in pregnancy or while breastfeeding. The trials excluded people who were pregnant, as is usual for this class.

**Who should not take it, as things stand:**

- **Nobody should take it outside a legitimate clinical trial.** It is not a licensed medicine. Lilly says no one can legally sell it for human use. The MHRA and the US FDA have both warned against products sold online as retatrutide, “retatrutide peptide” or “research use only” vials. In October 2026 the MHRA and West Midlands Police seized more than 12,000 fake retatrutide and tirzepatide pens, plus packaging for around 30,000 more, from a warehouse in Birmingham. The MHRA said those products have not been assessed for safety, quality or effectiveness.

Because there is no licensed product information, there is no official UK “do not take” list for retatrutide itself. For **already licensed** UK medicines in the same family (for example [Wegovy (semaglutide)](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/13801/smpc), last updated 22 September 2026, and [Mounjaro (tirzepatide)](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/15482/smpc), last updated 8 September 2026):

- The formal contraindication is **allergy** to the medicine or its ingredients.
- They **should not be used in pregnancy or while breastfeeding**. Women who could become pregnant are advised to use contraception. Wegovy should be stopped at least two months before a planned pregnancy; Mounjaro at least one month before.
- Caution is advised in people with a **history of pancreatitis**, and in people with **severe stomach-emptying problems (gastroparesis)**.
- Animal studies of this class have found thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents. The official UK information for Wegovy says the relevance to humans is considered low but cannot be completely ruled out.

These points are about similar licensed medicines, not a formal retatrutide label. A GP or specialist would need to judge any future use if the drug is ever licensed.

The two knee-pain trials also did not include people with diabetes, people of healthy weight, or people already needing a knee replacement, so the results do not automatically apply to those groups.

## 5. UK availability, NHS access and cost

**Retatrutide is not available as a legal medicine in the UK.**

- The **MHRA** said on 15 October 2026: “Retatrutide has not been authorised for use in the UK. Products being sold as retatrutide outside legitimate clinical trials are being supplied illegally and could pose serious risks to public health.”
- Lilly’s own FAQ, last updated in October 2026, says retatrutide “has not been reviewed or approved by the FDA or any regulatory agency in the world, and no one can legally sell it for human use.”
- There is therefore **no legitimate private UK prescription**, and **no official UK price**. Lilly has not announced a launch price. This note does not quote prices for illegal online products, because those products are unregulated and may not contain retatrutide at all.

**NHS / NICE:** NICE has selected retatrutide for a technology appraisal, [“Retatrutide for managing overweight and obesity” (ID6644, GID-TA11845)](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/indevelopment/gid-ta11845). The suggested remit is to assess it, alongside a healthy diet and physical activity, for obesity or for overweight with at least one weight-related condition, **once it has a marketing authorisation**. The appraisal is in development. The expected publication date is still “TBC”. After a request from the company, NICE said on 26 October 2026 that the appraisal is now expected to **begin in mid-December 2026**. Until the medicine is licensed and NICE (or NHS England) issues guidance, there are **no NHS access criteria** and it cannot be prescribed on the NHS.

**Possible timing, which could change:** On 23 July 2026 Lilly said it plans to submit a Biologics Licence Application to the **US FDA in the first quarter of 2027**. It also said it then had the clinical package to support **global** submissions for obesity, knee osteoarthritis pain and obstructive sleep apnoea. A filing is not an approval. No confirmed MHRA submission date or UK launch date was found. Even after a US filing, UK licensing and a NICE decision would usually take further months, sometimes longer.

Legal weekly weight-loss injections that are already licensed in the UK, such as Wegovy (semaglutide) and Mounjaro (tirzepatide), are a separate matter. They are not retatrutide, and this note does not treat their private-clinic prices as a stand-in for a drug that is not on the market.

**Main sources:** [MHRA, 15 October 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/counterfeit-weight-loss-pens-worth-2-million-seized-by-mhra-and-west-mids-police); [Lilly FAQ, October 2026](https://www.lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide); [NICE ID6644 project page](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/indevelopment/gid-ta11845); [NICE timeline note, 26 October 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/gid-ta11845/documents/html-content-2); [Lilly press release, 23 July 2026](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-successful-two-additional).

## 6. Caveats and limitations

- **Lilly funded both trials** and took part in the trial design, data analysis and manuscript preparation. Several authors are Lilly employees or have disclosed ties to the company. The *NEJM* paper states this openly.
- The ScienceDaily and Northwestern accounts are press summaries, not the full paper. Where a rounded phrase such as “about 25%” or “two-thirds” is used in the news coverage, this note prefers the exact published figures.
- The trials were **not designed** to show whether retatrutide slows the underlying arthritis, prevents the need for a knee replacement, or works in people who are not overweight. Those remain open questions. Pain was first formally scored at week 16, so the early time course is less clear. Inflammation markers were only measured at the start and end.
- There was **no direct comparison** in these trials with painkillers, steroid injections, or already licensed weight-loss injections such as semaglutide or tirzepatide.
- The **efficacy** figures (for people who stayed on treatment) look more impressive than the **intention-to-treat** figures. Both are real, but they answer different questions. People thinking about a future treatment should give more weight to the results that include those who stopped.
- Stopping rates because of side effects were not trivial at the higher doses, especially 12 mg in TRIUMPH-4 (18.2%).
- Results in people **without diabetes** and with a high starting BMI may not match results in other groups. In other retatrutide trials, people with type 2 diabetes generally lost a smaller percentage of their weight.
- This briefing was prepared on 21 November 2026 from publicly available sources. Licensing status, NICE timelines and safety information can change.

---

## Medical advice

This briefing is not medical advice. It does not recommend that anyone take retatrutide, buy an unlicensed product, or change an existing prescription. Anyone with knee pain, or who is thinking about a weight-loss medicine, should discuss the options with their GP. Licensed UK alternatives already exist for some people who meet NHS or private-clinic criteria; unlicensed “retatrutide” sold online should be avoided.
